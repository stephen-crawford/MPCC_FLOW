To demonstrate the **core contribution (OT mode learning reduces scenario mismatch under switching)**, you want tests that isolate *distribution shift* as the failure mode of standard scenario MPC — and then show OT fixes it **without blowing up conservatism or runtime**.

Below is a tight test plan I’d actually run/write up, with the few metrics that will make reviewers happy.

## 1) The three experiments that best “prove the point”

### A. Mode-switch distribution shift stress test (synthetic, controlled)

**Goal:** show baseline scenario MPC violates the intended risk bound when the obstacle switches modes inside the safe horizon, and OT restores bounded risk.

**Setup**

* 2–4 obstacle “modes” with very different trajectory statistics (e.g., straight, braking, lateral cut-in, zig-zag).
* Switching process is **non-Markov / adversarial-ish**: switch probability increases near intersections or near ego proximity.
* Predictors at time (t) only “see” the current mode unless you add OT/history.

**Compare (ablation)**

1. **Base**: scenario MPCC with predictor (P_t) (no OT, no extremes).
2. **History-only**: add past-realized extremes as hard constraints, but no OT reshaping.
3. **OT-only**: OT reshaping (history mixture), no extremes injection.
4. **OT + extremes**: your full method.

**Report**

* Empirical joint collision probability over horizon (Monte Carlo):
  (\hat p_{\text{coll}}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \mathbf{1}[\text{collision in horizon}])
* Constraint violation rate vs. target (\epsilon), ideally with a binomial CI.
* “Missed mode” rate: fraction of test rollouts where the realized mode is absent from sampling support (baseline will be high).

**Why reviewers buy it:** it’s a clean causal test: switching breaks i.i.d.-representativeness; OT restores support.

---

### B. Rare-mode / tail-event test (“needle in haystack”)

**Goal:** show OT meaningfully improves safety when the dangerous behavior is rare.

**Setup**

* One “danger mode” occurs with probability 1–5% but causes near-certain collision if not anticipated.
* Base predictor under-samples it.

**Report**

* Collision rate conditioned on rare mode occurrence (this is huge):
  (\hat p(\text{coll} \mid \text{rare mode}))
* False conservatism: average clearance, average speed/progress.

**Why it’s compelling:** it’s exactly the regime where a mode-history OT mechanism is supposed to help.

---

### C. Real-time / tractability test under fixed safety target

**Goal:** show you didn’t “buy safety with infinite computation.”

**Report (per MPC step)**

* solve time distribution (median, p90, p99)
* feasibility rate
* number of active constraints after pruning
* how performance changes with (S) (scenario count) and (S_{\text{ext}})

**Plot:** safety vs. runtime Pareto (baseline vs OT variants).

## 3) Metrics that actually show “OT mode learning” worked

### Safety / chance-constraint fidelity

* **Empirical joint collision probability** over safe horizon (the headline).
* **Empirical violation probability** of half-space constraints (cheaper proxy).
* **Calibration plot:** predicted risk vs observed risk (bin into predicted-risk deciles).

### Distributional mismatch (most directly tied to OT)

You want to quantify “sampling distribution matches realized behavior better.”

Pick one:

* **Wasserstein-2 distance** between predicted trajectory distribution and empirical realized trajectories in (\mathbb{R}^{2N_s}).
* **Energy distance / MMD** (easy with samples; no density needed).
* **Coverage metric:** fraction of realized trajectories that fall inside the ((1-\rho)) predicted region (e.g., ellipsoidal region in whitened space), before/after OT.

Even a single plot like:
**Mismatch metric vs time under switching** (baseline spikes; OT stays flat)
is extremely persuasive.

### Conservatism / performance

* contouring error, lag error
* average speed/progress
* minimum clearance margin (or distribution of min clearance)
* control smoothness

### Efficiency

* constraints active at optimum (support constraints)
* pruning ratio
* solve time

## 4) The best statistical tests to back the claims

You don’t need fancy stats, just correct ones:

* **Binomial proportion CI** for collision/violation rates (Wilson interval).
* **McNemar’s test** if you run paired rollouts with same random seeds (collision yes/no differs across methods).
* **Mann–Whitney U** for solve time distributions (non-Gaussian).
* **Permutation test** for mismatch metrics (W2/MMD), if you want to be robust.

Also: always report **number of rollouts** and seeds.

## 7) Small but important implementation checks

These are “sanity tests” that prevent reviewers from poking holes:

* **Non-anticipativity check:** extremes come only from past realized data, not from current sample batch.
* **Ablate OT map quality:** compare exact OT (if small) vs entropic OT approximation—show similar safety.
* **Buffer size sensitivity:** show OT still works for small history (e.g., 20–50 trajectories).
